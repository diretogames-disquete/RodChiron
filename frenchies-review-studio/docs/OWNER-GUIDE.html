Your Reviews, Answered in Your Voice

The owner's guide — plain language, no tech background needed.

Why this matters (the 2-minute pitch)

When you reply to a review, you're not really writing to that one client. You're writing to the next 50 people who read it before they decide whether to book.

Your review section is a sales page you don't control — but your replies are the part you do. A warm, specific, human reply says "real people work here, they pay attention, and they care." A copy-pasted "Thank you for your feedback!" says the opposite — it reads like a robot, and everyone can tell.

The problem: writing a genuinely good, different reply to every review takes time and energy you don't have after a full day on the floor. So most owners skip it or paste the same line every time.

This tool fixes that. You paste in a review, and in a few seconds it gives you 2–4 genuinely different reply options written in your salon's voice — each with a one-line note on why that angle works — so you just pick one, tweak a word if you want, and post it. It's the difference between a blank page and a short, easy decision.

What makes it different from generic "AI reply" tools:

  • It sounds like you, not a robot. Your voice, salon facts, and technicians are built in. It opens by reacting to what the reviewer actually said — never "Thank you for your feedback."
  • It gives you a real choice. Two to four different strategies, not the same sentence reworded.
  • It protects you. Nasty reviews, legal/BBB threats, or anything about an injury get flagged "needs your sign-off" so you slow down and handle them carefully.
  • It never makes things up. No invented prices, no fake enthusiasm, no promises you didn't make.
  • You control the cost. Replies cost roughly a penny each (often less), and you choose which AI it uses.

Nothing is ever posted automatically. You always pick and can edit first.

What you'll need

  • A computer (Mac or Windows).
  • About 10 minutes to set it up once.
  • One "API key." Think of it like a prepaid arcade card for the AI: you get a key from an AI company, and each reply spends a tiny bit of credit (about a penny or less). We'll walk through it.

Setting it up (pick the easy path)

Path A — The double-click app (recommended)

1. Install Node.js once: go to nodejs.org, download the green “LTS” button, run the installer.

2. Get the app folder onto your computer.

3. Double-click start.command (Mac) or start.bat (Windows).

4. A small window opens and your browser pops up at the app. Keep that window open while you use it.

Path B — The single file (no install)

1. Get the file standalone.html onto your computer.

2. Double-click it. It opens in your browser. That's it.

Works best with an Anthropic, Google, or OpenRouter key. If an OpenAI key won't connect, use Path A or switch providers — the app will tell you.

macOS note for Path A: the first time, if your Mac says start.command is blocked, right-click it → Open → Open.

Getting your AI key

Click "API keys" (top-right of the app). Pick one provider to start — any of these work well:

  • Anthropic (Claude) — great writing quality. Sign up at console.anthropic.com, add a little credit, create a key.
  • Google (Gemini) — very cheap. Get a key at aistudio.google.com/app/apikey.
  • OpenRouter — one key that reaches many models, good for low cost. Get one at openrouter.ai/keys (in the app, choose the "OpenAI-compatible" row and paste https://openrouter.ai/api/v1 as the base URL).

Paste the key, click Save, make sure that provider is selected (the dot), and close the window. You're ready.

Is my key safe? Yes. In the double-click app, your key stays on your own computer and is never shown back to you (only the last 4 digits). In the single file, it's saved in your own browser on your own device. Just don't email the file to other people with your key saved in it.

Your first reply (60 seconds)

  1. Paste the review into the big box.
  2. Click the stars to match the rating.
  3. If the review names one of your techs, pick them from Technician (or type the name).
  4. Click Generate responses.
  5. Read the little "Why this angle" under each option and pick your favorite. Tweak a word or two if you want it to sound even more like you.
  6. Click Copy, go to Google/Yelp/Facebook, and paste it as your reply.

That's the whole loop. After a couple of tries it takes under a minute per review.

Getting the most out of it

These habits turn "replying to reviews" into real marketing:

  1. Reply to every review, and do it fast. Within a day or two. Fresh replies signal an attentive business. This removes the "I don't know what to write" excuse.
  2. Reply to the bad ones calmly — that's the real win. A graceful, non-defensive reply to a 2-star review impresses readers more than ten 5-star replies. You're writing for the next 50 readers, not to win the argument. The tool is built for this — and flags the truly nasty or legal ones.
  3. Name your technicians — and give each one a personality. When a reviewer names Rheanna or Amber, the reply celebrates them by name. In Settings ▸ Salon & voice every technician now has three fields: her name, an archetype (how the voice frames her — "meticulous by nature," "the first-visit converter"), and a private note the model uses for context but never quotes. Add new hires, remove departures, tune how each is talked about — it's all yours.
  4. Keep your salon's identity current. In Settings ▸ Salon & voice, set your salon name, city, your own name as owner (the tough replies sign from you personally), your real price, and your sign-off. When someone grumbles about cost, the tool gently frames the value — never argues the number.
  5. Make the playbook yours. Settings ▸ Review cases shows all 22 situations the tool knows how to handle — each with the strategy it follows and who has to approve the reply. Everything is editable: rename a case, tighten a strategy, change who signs off, delete cases you never see, or add your own (walk-in complaints, no-show disputes, whatever your salon actually gets). New cases behave exactly like built-in ones — they show up in the dropdown, drive the reply strategy, and enforce their approval level. One-click Reset brings back the original 22 anytime.
  6. "Save as exemplar" the replies you love. The tool learns from those and gets more "you" over time.
  7. Turn raves into marketing. For standout reviews, one option is a "testimonial invitation" — a gracious way to ask permission to feature their words.
  8. Follow the colored approval chip. Every reply now comes with a small chip that tells you who should look at it before it posts, straight from your response playbook: Auto (green) — anyone trained on the voice can post it after a read · Check (amber) — a second pair of eyes first · Owner (red) — you read and approve personally; all negatives land here · Legal (purple) — you plus counsel or franchise support; injury and discrimination claims land here, no exceptions. Red- and purple-chip replies always carry the "needs your sign-off" banner and are marked as drafts.
  9. Read the "Deliberately left out" box. Under the replies, the tool lists what it chose not to say — no refund talk, no arguing the timeline, no naming the tech in a complaint — and the risk each omission avoids. If you edit a reply, don't add those things back in. And when a "fragile wording" warning appears (it will on injury claims), it tells you which passage not to trim — edit it longer, never shorter.
  10. Keep costs tiny. The little line above the replies shows about how much each one cost. Use a cheap model for everyday replies (pick it in "Run this with"); save the premium one for high-stakes reviews.

A simple weekly routine (about 10 minutes)

  1. Open the app (double-click the launcher, or the file).
  2. Open Google/Yelp/Facebook and find reviews you haven't answered.
  3. For each: paste it in → set stars → pick the tech → Generate → pick → tweak → Copy → paste as your reply.
  4. Any red-flagged ones: set aside and handle carefully (or with help).

Ten minutes a week keeps your review section looking alive and human.

Do's and don'ts

Do

• Read every reply before you post it — make it sound like you.

• Answer the negatives; they're your biggest opportunity.

• Keep your price and technician list up to date.

• Save the replies you love so the voice keeps improving.

Don't

• Don't post without reading (it never posts for you — on purpose).

• Don't argue with an unhappy client in public; own what's fair, take specifics to a private message.

• Don't paste a client's private personal details.

• Don't share the single-file version with your key saved in it.

Common questions

Does it post replies for me?

No — never. You always copy and paste it yourself, so you're in full control.

What does it cost to run?

Roughly a penny per reply on the top-quality AI, and a fraction of a cent on the budget ones. You pay the AI company directly; the app itself is free.

Where does my key/data go?

Your key stays on your device. The reviews you paste are sent to the AI you chose (just like typing into any AI tool) to write the reply.

What if a review names a technician I don't have?

The tool notices and quietly asks if you want to add that name to your roster — it won't invent a person.

Can my front-desk person use it?

Yes. It's simple enough for anyone on your team to run the weekly routine. Just make sure they read replies before posting, especially the flagged ones.

Can I move my setup to another computer?

In the single-file version, use Settings ▸ Salon & voice ▸ Export voice library to save your settings and favorite replies, then Import them on the other computer. (Your key isn't included — re-enter that once.)
