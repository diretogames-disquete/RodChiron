# House Voice — Approved Exemplars

These are real, approved responses that anchor the house voice. The app loads the
**most recent** exemplars from this file as few-shot examples (capped so the
prompt stays lean). When you approve or edit a response in the app and click
**"Save as exemplar,"** it is appended to the bottom of this file — so the voice
sharpens with use. Newest wins; feel free to prune older or weaker blocks.

Format note: each exemplar is a block that begins with a `## ` heading. Keep that
shape so the app can parse them.

## Exemplar 1 — Short positive, technician named
> **Review (5★):** "Love my mani! Amber is fab!"
> **Response:** "She really is. Thanks for the love — Amber will be thrilled to see this, and we'll see you next time!"
> **Moves:** matches the six-word energy; opens by *agreeing* rather than thanking; names Amber personally; a light rebook close.

## Exemplar 2 — Detailed positive, technician named, product-curious
> **Review (5★):** "Very impressed with pedicure — my best ever! Rheanna was thorough, used products I haven't had used on my feet. Polish looks great and my feet feel amazing!"
> **Response:** "\"My best ever\" is a high bar and we're honored to hold it. Rheanna is meticulous by nature, and noticing the products is a real compliment — we're intentional about what we use and how we care for the natural nail, so it means a lot that you felt the difference. Enjoy those feet, and come see us again soon."
> **Moves:** anchors on the quoted key phrase; gives Rheanna a character trait; weaves the natural-nail differentiator as a light aside, not a pitch.

## Exemplar 3 — Short positive, no technician, values-anchored
> **Review (5★):** "Clean, friendly staff, great products!"
> **Response:** "Clean listed first — that's the one we work hardest for. Thank you for noticing all three."
> **Moves:** no tech to name, so it anchors on the content; homes in on "clean" (the brand thesis); two sentences, no sales ask; restraint as confidence.

## Exemplar 4 — First-visit convert, no technician, plays off the reviewer's humor
> **Review (5★):** "I'm a 72-year-old guy and it's getting harder to trim my toenails. Frenchies was recommended for my father-in-law but I thought I would go ahead 'for him.' It was worth it. I'll be back."
> **Response:** "Coming in \"for him\" — your secret's safe with us. Honestly, this is exactly the kind of care that gets harder to do for yourself and shouldn't have to be, so we're glad you talked yourself into it. \"I'll be back\" might be our favorite three words in any review — see you then."
> **Moves:** plays along with his joke (matches warmth); acknowledges his real reason without leaning on his age or turning clinical; deliberately omits any "men are welcome" or sterilization talk that would other him; keeps his dignity fully intact.

## Exemplar 5 — Membership convert (Case 06): the joining line is the lede
> **Review (5★):** "…The facility is cleaner than clean! …I became a member afterwards… Amber took care of me and she's amazing!"
> **Response:** "\"Cleaner than clean\" might be the highest compliment we know how to earn — that one means the most to us. What really makes our day, though, is that you walked out a member: that's someone deciding on day one that this is their place, and we don't take it lightly. Amber will be so glad she was the one who showed you. Welcome in — we've got you from here."
> **Moves:** the membership decision outranks the louder compliments and takes the lede; no benefits pitch — the proof is their choice; Amber gets a warm supporting beat, not the lede; closes as a welcome, not a thank-you.

## Exemplar 6 — Policy dispute (Case 15): who the rule protects
> **Review (1★):** "I made it to my appointment, walking in at 3:05… She then told me I was nearly 20 minutes late… and that it had already been canceled… Completely ruined my wellness day."
> **Response:** "Thank you for taking the time to write, and I'm sorry your day was disrupted — that's genuinely not how I want anyone to leave here. On the timing: our arrival window is short by design, and I want to be honest about why. Every appointment is booked back to back, and when one start slides, it doesn't just shorten that service — it pushes into the appointment of the client sitting in our lobby who arrived on time. Protecting her afternoon is the same standard that protects yours when you're the one on time. That window is posted in the salon and shown at booking, and I do hold to it, because a rule I only enforce sometimes isn't fair to anybody. My door's open if you'd like to talk it through. — Kira"
> **Moves:** never says "you were late"; states the standard by who it protects (the next client), converting the reader from juror to sympathizer; "a rule I only enforce sometimes isn't fair to anybody" signals the window is real without scolding; no timeline correction, no fine print, no charge/refund talk; owner signs personally.

## Exemplar 7 — Injury claim (Case 17): the nail-bed paragraph (fragile — edit longer, not shorter)
> **Review (2★, excerpt):** "…the stylist made my nail bed bleed and burned another nail in the process."
> **Response (the health passage):** "On your earlier visit — I'd really like to hear more. Nail beds can become tender or bleed for a number of reasons that aren't always about technique: certain medications, thyroid or circulation conditions, very dry or overworked cuticles, or how the nails are cared for between visits. That's not me dismissing what you felt, it's me saying I'd want to look at it properly with you and with your doctor if it's ongoing, because we build our whole approach around the health of the natural nail."
> **Moves:** reopens as an invitation instead of rebutting; alternative causes offered only as possibilities that *include our own work*; every cause hedged; routes to the doctor; lands on the natural-nail philosophy. **Fragility:** trimmed short, or with the doctor referral removed, it reads as blame-shifting — edit it longer, never shorter. Scope limit: this framing fits tenderness/bleeding, never a burn, cut, or instrument-traced infection.
